What a night actually costs, and what it is worth.

Only one property publishes a date-specific itemised checkout total, and only through its own booking engine: The Venetian. It priced the same two-night stay at $1,245.48 on 2026-11-16 and at $1,015.89 on 2026-11-17, and for the recommended 23–30 November 2026 trip stay it printed $4,010.55 (and $3,823.46 for a Palazzo suite 20 minutes later, F78) on 2026-11-17. On 2026-11-19 the engine first returned only a loading shell (F93) and then, on a later read that day, printed a full itemised total for the identical query — $3,619.39 for a Palazzo Luxury King (F110, and the all-in per night is that printed total ÷ 7 = $517.06) — the clearest argument there is for keeping a dated log instead of one number. What every property does publish is the pieces: a room rate or published starting rate where available, a mandatory daily resort fee, a tax rule, a parking price, a cancellation deadline and the terms for holding a booking. This page puts those pieces in the same columns so the properties can be compared like for like, and it appends every observation rather than overwriting it. Walking times come from Google Maps walking estimates captured on 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-17 (each row carries its own measurement date), because no property publishes a walking time to its own poker room.

Recommendation

Fixed by this session; the reasoning is published rather than asked for.

Recommended

The Venetian or The Palazzo poker suite

Both properties are on the same resort as the main poker room. The official poker-room rate page publishes starting rates of $119 and $209 and requires six hours of live cash play or a $300 or higher tournament entry for each night stayed, completed before 2 a.m. on the last night. The mandatory $55 resort fee and 13.38% Clark County room tax take the floor to $197.28 a night. The Palazzo is a four-minute walk from the Venetian poker room.

Both figures are published starting rates, not a quote for your dates, and the page does not map each starting rate to a named tower. That is why the room-rate column shows a range.

Quality floor

What “good enough” means here

No reviewed property publishes a comparable quality score, so the quality column is blank rather than invented. The floor applied instead is evidence-based: the property must publish its own poker-room rate, keep the poker room on site, and publish its resort fee, tax and cancellation terms. On that test the Venetian and the Palazzo are the only properties that qualify, and both are the same resort.

Wynn publishes the clearest fee and cancellation terms of any property reviewed, and its rooms page links Forbes Travel Guide, but it publishes no room price, so no total can be computed.

What still has to be confirmed

  • Your dates, number of nights and number of guests. Every rate is date- and occupancy-dependent.
  • The room-tax percentage on the properties’ own pages for Wynn, Bellagio, MGM Grand, Caesars Palace, Horseshoe and ARIA: each publishes “plus applicable tax” without the percentage, so property-published true nightly costs stay blank for them (F39; Wynn carries a separately labelled derived figure from its dated fee-inclusive engine averages).
  • A single nightly parking constant for Horseshoe Las Vegas: caesars.com/parking publishes $20/day registered-guest self-parking at Caesars Palace ($50/day valet), while Horseshoe splits registered-guest self-parking by weekday ($20/day Mon–Wed, $25/day Thu–Sun; $40/day valet), so no flat single-night constant is stored (F102).
  • Whether the $119 / $209 poker rate is quoted before or after the $55 resort fee. The official Resort Fee page says “Rates include a daily Resort Fee of $55 plus tax per night” — which is the reading the $197.28 floor uses and makes it correct arithmetic — while the booking calendar says its own prices include the fee. One official page says the fee is added, the other says it is included, so the floor keeps the conservative reading and overstates the nightly cost by about $43 if the poker rate turns out to be fee-inclusive — see F51.
  • Calendar cells vs itemised quotes: Horseshoe Las Vegas and MGM Grand published dated calendar cells for the trip window (already including their resort fees; two Horseshoe readings five minutes apart moved, F55), and Wynn’s engine printed dated pre-tax averages ($495 / $405 avg/night incl. resort fee) on 2026-11-19, whereas only The Venetian printed an itemised checkout total.
  • Whether the poker-suite rate is available on your dates; the Venetian states that rates are provided up to 150 days out.
  • The poker rate for the trip dates themselves. The Venetian engine priced the recommended window at $4,010.55 all-in on 2026-11-17 (flagged F66/F69) and at $3,619.39 on 2026-11-19 (F110), but both are the engine's best-available rate, and the $119/$209 poker rate is booked only by phone under code EPOKR25, so no dated total for that rate exists. Four other stay windows inside the earlier dates returned no suite and no price.
