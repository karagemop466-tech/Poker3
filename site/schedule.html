How the estimates work

Read this before trusting the conflict flags.

Estimates

Where the finish time comes from

No venue publishes a finish time. When a structure sheet prints the late-registration cut-off as a clock time, the session cannot have finished before it, so the planner adds two more levels and calls that the estimated finish. Where no cut-off time is printed, the planner uses a stated six-hour session and adds nothing else.

Transfers

Thirty minutes between rooms

Two events conflict when the second starts before the first’s estimated finish plus a 30-minute transfer. The transfer figure is a planning allowance for the Strip, not a measured crossing time; the walking estimates on the hotels page come from Google Maps and do not model Strip traffic, indoor walkways or crowds.

Recurring rows

Why the daily rooms are not the primary

The Venetian and Wynn series rows are dated, so they can be placed on a calendar. The Caesars, Horseshoe, MGM Grand, Bellagio and ARIA tournaments are published as a weekly or daily cadence, so they are listed for each weekday they run rather than as dated events. The Caesars add-on and the MGM Grand, Bellagio and Wynn re-entry terms mean those rows belong to the extra-spend section, not to the single-entry path.

What the plan is not

Not a guarantee, and not a forecast

A published guarantee is a floor paid only if the field fills. The pool ÷ fee figure is descriptive. The bankroll figure is the sum of the recommended buy-ins plus a 25% buffer. None of these numbers predicts an outcome, and the room’s own confirmation on the day overrides every row on this page.
