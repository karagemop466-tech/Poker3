F01 medium Hotel cost

Horseshoe resort fee is printed two ways on the same official page

The price breakdown on the official booking page uses a $50 daily resort fee, while the fee policy section of the same page prints 'Resort fees: $56.63 ($49.95 plus $6.68 tax)'. The two figures cannot both be the charge for the same stay.

'$223 Room Rate + $50 Daily Resort Fee = $273 / Night' against 'Resort fees: $56.63 ($49.95 plus $6.68 tax)'.

Why it matters: The true nightly cost on this site uses the price-breakdown figure ($273 x 1.1338 = $309.53) because that is the figure the booking engine itself uses. If the policy line is the charge, the true nightly is $309.53 rather than $309.53 + $7.53.

What to do: Confirm the resort fee on the checkout summary for the exact stay before paying.

Sources: Horseshoe Las Vegas - official booking engine (Dec 10-14, 2026, 2 adults)

F13 high Scheduling

No venue publishes tournament finish times

Official structure sheets give start time, blind levels, level length and the time registration closes, but no finish time. A tournament can run past its registration cut-off by hours.

Why it matters: The overlap check on this site uses the published registration-close time as a hard lower bound on event length. A pair is flagged 'cannot be sequenced' only when the second event starts before the first event's registration closed, which is provable; pairs that start inside the first event's possible playing window are flagged 'uncertain' instead.

What to do: Ask each room for the expected duration of the events on your shortlist, then re-run the check.

Sources: The Venetian - DeepStack Extravaganza IV structure sheets 1-37 (one PDF per sheet)

F19 medium Analysis

Guaranteed pool divided by entry fee is a description, not a forecast

A guarantee is the venue's promise about the prize pool, not the expected payout. Bounty guarantees are larger because they include bounty money that is paid only for eliminations, so a bounty event's ratio is not comparable with a plain freezeout's.

Why it matters: This site prints both the guarantee-to-buy-in ratio and the cash-pool-to-buy-in ratio (the latter from the sheet's own fee split) and labels bounty, satellite, multi-flight and team events separately.

What to do: Use the cash-pool ratio when comparing a bounty event with a freezeout.

Sources: The Venetian - DeepStack Extravaganza IV structure sheets 1-37 (one PDF per sheet)

F20 medium Tournament terms

PokerAtlas PRO discounts and early-registration rake discounts change the price of some events

Events #17, #18, #24, #25, #46, #48 and #58 are tagged '@PokerAtlas PRO' on the official schedule, and the Event #18 sheet says 'All subsequent buy in's after the initial buy in will be $200'. Event #56 gives a $40 rake discount for Day 1A entries before 10:10 a.m. and Day 1B entries before 6:10 p.m.; Event #59 gives a $20 discount for Day 1A entries before 11:10 a.m.

'PokerAtlas PRO players will receive a twenty ($20) rake discount on their initial $200 buy in only ... All subsequent buy in's after the initial buy in will be $200.'

Why it matters: Event #18 and #58 are excluded from the single-entry list because the sheet itself contemplates further buy-ins. The rake discounts are a cost advantage for early or member registration, not extra spend, so those events stay eligible but carry a note.

What to do: Decide whether you will be at the registration desk before the cut-off time.

F24 high Hotel rate

Rates are a dated log, not a single price

Hotel rates in this dashboard are stored as dated observations (property, check date, arrival, departure, occupancy, room type, rate, whether the resort fee is included, whether tax is included, rooms found). The same property can print several prices on the same day, and the price moves with demand.

Why it matters: Any total that quotes a single rate is a snapshot of 2026-11-16, not a quote for the traveller's dates.

What to do: Add a new dated observation each time you re-check, and compare like-for-like: same dates, same occupancy, same room type, same tax treatment.

No property publishes a star rating for a room. The quality column on this site uses the published size of the cheapest listed room as a like-for-like proxy, and it is labelled as a proxy wherever it appears.
